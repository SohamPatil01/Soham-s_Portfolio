Engineering Case Study — 01

An offline-first POS that never stops billing

ClientK2 Chicken — franchise retail
TimelineDec 2025 — Feb 2026
RoleFreelance full-stack developer
StackReact · Next.js · Node.js · IndexedDB
StatusIn production, 3 locations
The Problem

When the internet goes down, everything stops.

The franchise operates in areas with frequent power outages — 4 to 6 hours of no internet is routine. With traditional POS systems, that means:

  • No way to process payments — customers leave
  • Inventory records go out of sync
  • Sales data vanishes until the connection returns

The real requirement was simple: the counter needs to keep working whether the internet exists or not.

The Solution

Treat offline as the default state, not the failure state.

I built the system with offline as the starting point: every transaction goes to IndexedDB in the browser first, instantly, regardless of connection. A background sync engine watches for the network and reconciles the queue automatically — cashiers don't have to think about it.

The full system handles inventory management, billing, customer profiles, loyalty programs, sales reports, and role-based access for all three locations.

Challenges & Trade-offs
Challenge 01

How do you prevent data conflicts when the connection returns?

Three stores syncing hours of queued transactions at once is a recipe for collisions. I resolved it with timestamp-based sync and explicit conflict-resolution rules — local transactions carry their creation time, and the sync engine reconciles against the cloud record deterministically instead of last-write-wins guesswork.

Challenge 02

How do you keep inventory honest across three stores?

Manual stock counts were the original source of drift. I built automated stock tracking and reconciliation workflows into the sync layer, so inventory adjusts from actual transactions rather than end-of-day memory — measurably reducing discrepancies.

Challenge 03

How much system do you give a non-technical cashier?

Franchise staff are not developers. Role-based access control keeps the counter view simple — billing and customers — while owners see inventory, loyalty and sales reporting. The trade-off is more upfront design; the payoff is software people actually use.

Lessons Learned
Building offline-first taught me that reliability often matters more than new features. Synchronization and edge cases demanded more careful design than anything visible in the UI. — The part of the system users never see is the part they depend on most
