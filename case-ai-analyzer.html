AI that tells a business what technology it actually needs

The Problem

Small businesses know something is broken. They don't know what to buy.

Digital transformation advice is usually either generic ("get a website") or sold by whoever profits from the answer. A business owner's real questions — where am I losing money to manual process, and what tool fixes it — require research nobody has time to do: auditing the website, reading reviews, comparing against how similar businesses operate.

The Solution

Automate the research. Deliver a verdict, not a data dump.

The platform ingests a business's website, Google Business Profile and customer reviews, then runs the analysis through an orchestrated LLM layer using both OpenAI and Gemini. The output is a structured opportunity report: concrete recommendations like POS systems, appointment platforms, AI automation, inventory management and customer engagement tools — each tied to evidence found in the business's own footprint.

Around the AI core sit secure dashboards, REST APIs and lead management workflows, so the reports feed a real sales process rather than sitting in a folder.

Challenges & Trade-offs
Challenge 01

How do you get consistent, structured output from LLMs?

Free-form model output can't drive a dashboard. The recommendation layer constrains responses to structured report formats that the application validates before anything reaches the UI — treating the LLM as a component with a contract, not an oracle.

Challenge 02

Why orchestrate two model providers instead of one?

OpenAI and Gemini have different strengths and different failure modes. Running both adds integration cost, but buys resilience and quality — the platform isn't hostage to one provider's outages, rate limits or blind spots.

Challenge 03

How does analysis become revenue?

A report nobody acts on is trivia. Building lead management workflows directly into the platform turns each analysis into a tracked opportunity with a next step — the difference between a tech demo and a business tool.

Lessons Learned
The hard part of AI products isn't calling the model — it's everything around the call: structure, validation, and turning output into a workflow someone acts on. — AI is a component, the product is the system
